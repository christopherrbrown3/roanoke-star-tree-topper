# Roanoke Star Christmas Tree Topper — Two Colors, No Assembly

Bring a little of Roanoke's Mill Mountain to your Christmas tree. This 200 mm topper captures the landmark's layered neon appearance with **130 individual tube sections, three white star bands, and crisp dark outlines**. The star and its rear tree socket print together as one piece.

The white tubes and backing bands are flush inlays. Thin dark outlines keep each tube distinct, including the bends around the points and lower recesses. The back has recessed **christopherbrown.io** lettering.

## Print setup

- **Printer:** Bambu Lab A1 with AMS lite
- **Nozzle / layers:** 0.4 mm / 0.20 mm
- **Filaments:** two compatible PLA colors — black or navy for the body and outlines, white for the face details
- **Size:** 200 mm tall × 190.96 mm wide × 50.84 mm deep
- **Orientation:** decorated face against the build plate
- **Process:** four walls, five top and bottom layers, 15% gyroid infill, Arachne walls, prime tower enabled, supports disabled
- **Assembly:** none

Open the 3MF **as a project** in Bambu Studio and map filament 1 to your dark spool and filament 2 to white. The decorated face occupies the **first five layers**; view those layers in the sliced preview to see both colors. The full-height view looks mostly dark because the white face is against the bed. The included plate setting is Textured PEI; select the plate you actually use.

The A1 slice estimates **2 h 47 min and 70.5 g of PLA**, including purge and prime-tower material. Estimates vary with filament profiles and settings.

The integrated socket is 80 mm long with nominal circular clearance tapering from Ø34 to Ø22 mm. Rear diamond-shaped holes accept optional retaining ties. Check your tree leader against these dimensions before printing.

For the alternative STL download, import **both material files together as parts of one object**, preserve their shared position, and assign the two colors. The separate meshes form one finished print.

## A Christmas landmark since 1949

The Roanoke Merchants Association commissioned the Mill Mountain Star for the 1949 Christmas season. First illuminated on November 23, 1949, the 88.5-foot landmark became a permanent part of the city's skyline.

This model grew out of municipal and historical records, an original-plan search, and comparisons of more than fifteen photographs and views. The research considered the perspective distortion in photographs taken from below and used parallel offsets to keep the nested tube paths consistent. It is a photographic interpretation of the landmark; no original dimensioned shop drawing was located in the sources reviewed. The 130 tube sections describe this model's pattern.

The gallery includes photographs of the owner's completed two-color print with the three white backing bands. The festive cover is an **AI-generated product illustration** based on the model and print photographs; use the actual print photos to judge the surface finish.
