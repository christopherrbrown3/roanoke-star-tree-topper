# README history and research draft

The Roanoke/Mill Mountain Star was built for the 1949 Christmas season as a Roanoke Merchants Association promotion. Historical accounts identify Roy C. Kinsey and the Kinsey Sign Company as the sign builders, with the supporting structure associated with Roanoke Iron & Bridge Works and designer Robert R. Little (some accounts give the middle initial as L.; the sources are not uniform). A WDBJ historical transcript records that the full-scale star was laid out and assembled on the ground at Roanoke’s airport, in front of an unused hangar, then disassembled, moved to Mill Mountain, and reassembled on its tower. The City’s landmark page gives the first illumination as November 23, 1949, and describes the star as 88.5 feet high. See the [City of Roanoke history](https://www.roanokeva.gov/1329/Roanoke-Star), [WDBJ transcript preserved by Virginia Tech](https://scholar.lib.vt.edu/VA-news/WDBJ-7/script_archives/99/1199/112899/112899.m.htm), [1982 historical account](https://www.virginiaroom.org/digital/files/original/67/6354/JHSWV_11_02_1982.pdf), and [Virginia DHR nomination](https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/VLR_to_transfer/PDFNoms/128-0352_Roanoke_Star_1999_Final_Nomination.pdf).

The landmark’s appearance changes substantially with viewpoint and lighting mode. The City describes three nested structural frames carrying multiple neon sets. Nearly frontal all-white views support six dominant luminous contours arranged as three close pairs; that is a visible-pattern observation, not a count of every glass tube or electrical circuit. More than fifteen source photographs and views were compared across City captures, Schumin’s ground-level night series, and Wikimedia Commons. Schumin’s close photographs show two rows of tubing on the non-red rings, with many shorter sections and hardware at corners, notches, and outer tips. A colored Commons close-up exposes several apparent interruptions along a long horizontal run. Because the available photos are low-angle or oblique, foreshortening and support occlusion can move or hide a joint; candidate angles, exact break locations, and any universal stagger between paired runs remain unverified. The [Schumin close-up set](https://www.schuminweb.com/photography/roanoke-star-night/) and [colored Commons photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mill_Mountain_Star_Neon_Lights.JPG) are useful appearance references, not measured elevations.

Public dimensional references do not agree on width. The [Smithsonian inventory](https://siris-artinventories.si.edu/ipac20/ipac.jsp?booklistformat=&profile=ariall&session=1761P49582NO9.272492&uri=full%3D3100001~%21334963~%210) gives an approximate 88.5-by-84.5-foot star, while the [City art catalog](https://www.artworkarchive.com/profile/roanoke-arts/artwork/roanoke-star?collection=city-of-roanoke-permanent-collection) lists 1062 by 1200 inches (88.5 by 100 feet), without making the relevant axes clear. No public dimensioned elevation or original shop drawing was located in this review; that does not establish that none survives. The DHR PDF available online contains the nomination and selected location/map material but omits Exhibits B–F. The [DHR Archives](https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/programs/dhr-archives/) and [Roanoke library vertical-file finding aid](https://www.virginiaroom.org/digital/files/original/13/1792/VerticalFiles.2.pdf) are leads for requesting missing or oversized records, including Kinsey, Roanoke Iron & Bridge Works, and Mill Mountain files.

The current project therefore treats the star as a documented historical reference with an explicitly approximate reconstruction. The build is an offline, print-in-place two-color FDM interpretation for a Bambu A1 with AMS Lite: a dark main body and backing, an integral rear tree mount, and roughly 130 white inlay islands that make the three paired tube sections individually visible. It uses no LEDs, wiring, diffuser, or electronics. The three-pair topology is retained while section placement and candidate angles remain provisional pending better archival or face-on measurement evidence.
