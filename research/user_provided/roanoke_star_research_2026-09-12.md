# Roanoke Star Reconstruction Research

**Subject:** Roanoke Star / Mill Mountain Star, Roanoke, Virginia  
**Purpose:** Consolidated research for a geometrically faithful reproduction, especially an all-white 3D-printable version  
**Status:** Working research summary. Documented facts are separated from photo-derived inferences and rejected hypotheses.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The Roanoke Star is best understood as **three nested structural star frames** mounted on a steel support tower, with multiple neon tube runs attached to those frames.

For reproduction purposes, the most important visual finding is:

> **The all-white configuration shows six dominant illuminated star contours arranged as three close pairs.**

The spacing hierarchy is:

> **[two close lines] — larger gap — [two close lines] — larger gap — [two close lines]**

This is the single most important geometry correction from earlier attempts. The six visible contours should **not** be modeled as six evenly scaled stars.

The strongest documented overall dimensions found so far are:

- **Star height:** ~88 ft 6 in
- **Star width:** ~84 ft 6 in
- **Star depth:** ~2 in
- **Support tower height:** ~100 ft
- **Support width:** ~64 ft 10 in
- **Support depth:** ~29 ft 2 in
- **Foundation depth:** ~6 ft 6 in
- **Star weight:** ~10,000 lb
- **Support steel weight:** ~60,000 lb
- **Concrete foundation weight:** ~500,000 lb
- **Total neon tubing:** ~2,000 linear ft

The width measurement is especially important because it shows that the actual Star is **not a regular pentagram**. A mathematically regular five-point star that is 88.5 ft tall would be about 93.05 ft wide, substantially wider than the Smithsonian survey measurement of ~84.5 ft.

The most plausible current reproduction target is therefore:

- all-white lighting
- three structural star bands
- two prominent visible tube tracks per band
- large gaps between the three structural bands
- vertically elongated overall silhouette
- approximately 30°/60° edge families, based on photo measurement
- no reliance on decorative replicas or flight-simulator geometry

## 3. Overall geometry: not a regular pentagram

A regular upright five-point star with height 88.5 ft would have width:

\[
W/H \approx 1.051462
\]

So:

\[
88.5 \times 1.051462 \approx 93.054 \text{ ft}
\]

But the Smithsonian survey gives:

\[
W/H = 84.5 / 88.5 \approx 0.954802
\]

That is radically different.

### Conclusion

The Roanoke Star is **vertically elongated relative to a mathematically regular pentagram**.

This means the following should **not** be assumed:

- 36° regular-pentagram point angle
- golden-ratio inner radius
- regular five-fold rotational geometry
- equal radial scaling of all nested contours

The reproduction should instead use the actual photographed silhouette and documented envelope.

## 4. Three structural frames vs. visible illuminated loops

### Current City description

The City of Roanoke describes the installation as **three stars / three frames**:

- outer
- middle
- inner

Each frame contains multiple clear neon tube sets.

The City has described each frame as containing roughly **3–5 sets of clear neon tubes**.

Source:

- City of Roanoke, Roanoke Star  
  https://www.roanokeva.gov/1329/Roanoke-Star

### What the all-white photos show

The strongest all-white frontal photographs clearly show **six dominant bright star contours**.

The most useful interpretation is:

- **Outer structural frame:** 2 closely paired visible tube tracks
- **Middle structural frame:** 2 closely paired visible tube tracks
- **Inner structural frame:** 2 closely paired visible tube tracks

Therefore:

\[
3 \text{ structural bands} \times 2 \text{ visible bright tracks} = 6 \text{ visible loops}
\]

This is the correct working topology for the all-white reproduction.

### Important distinction

These six visible loops are **not the same thing as six continuous physical neon tubes**.

Each visible star track is made from many shorter fabricated glass tube segments, mounted in pieces around the star.

Also, the City's “3–5 sets per frame” language likely refers to fabricated tube sets / circuitry / grouped tube pieces, not only the six dominant front-view luminous contours.

## 5. White configuration vs. red/white/blue configuration

The all-white configuration is the best target for reproduction because it exposes the underlying geometry.

### All-white configuration

Observed:

- six visible luminous contours
- grouped as three pairs
- all six visible at once
- cleanest geometry reference

### RGB / patriotic configuration

Some historical and modern RGB photos appear to show only **five obvious illuminated outlines**.

One commonly described arrangement is approximately:

- 1 red outer outline
- 2 white middle outlines
- 2 blue inner outlines

This can produce a **1 + 2 + 2 = 5** visual count.

Possible reasons:

- one outer contour may not be energized in that configuration
- color grouping may visually merge nearby tube tracks
- exposure / glow may obscure one adjacent contour
- different circuits may energize different tube subsets

This is why RGB photographs should not be used as the primary tube-count reference.

### Reproduction choice

**Target the all-white configuration.**

## 6. Historical descriptions that conflict

Several historical descriptions count the Star differently.

### “Three stars”

Current City terminology: three structural frames.

### “Five concentric stars”

A 1999 WDBJ historical retrospective, drawing on older interviews, described the approved 1949 design as involving **five concentric stars**.

Source:

- WDBJ archival transcript via Virginia Tech  
  https://scholar.lib.vt.edu/VA-news/WDBJ-7/script_archives/99/1199/112899/112899.m.htm

### “Nine rows” of neon

Virginia Room postcard material refers to approximately **nine rows** of neon and ~2,000 ft of tubing.

This is not necessarily contradictory if:

- “star” means a visible luminous outline
- “frame” means a structural carrier
- “row” means a tube row mounted on a frame
- “tube set” means a fabricated/electrical grouping

These terms appear to have been used inconsistently over time.

## 7. Fabrication history

A very useful historical account says the Star was assembled **flat on the ground at the Roanoke airport**, in front of an unused hangar.

The steel was:

1. laid out full scale
2. punched / fabricated
3. assembled for fit
4. disassembled
5. transported up Mill Mountain
6. reassembled on the tower

This matters because it strongly implies the original builders had either:

- dimensioned shop drawings
- full-scale lofting geometry
- templates
- or all of the above

The exact original blueprint has not yet been found online.

Source:

- WDBJ historical transcript  
  https://scholar.lib.vt.edu/VA-news/WDBJ-7/script_archives/99/1199/112899/112899.m.htm

## 14. Photo-derived tube spacing

Measurements from the user-provided all-white frontal image showed six bright centerlines grouped into three pairs.

On one useful cross-section, approximate pixel positions were:

- Outer pair: ~155, 168
- Middle pair: ~195, 212
- Inner pair: ~240, 256

Approximate pair spacings:

- 13 px
- 17 px
- 16 px

Average:

\[
\approx 15.3 \text{ px}
\]

Approximate inter-band gaps:

- ~27 px
- ~28 px

Average:

\[
\approx 27.5 \text{ px}
\]

So:

\[
\frac{\text{inter-band gap}}{\text{in-band spacing}} \approx 1.8
\]

### Working spacing rule

> **The clear gap between structural star bands is roughly 1.8× the spacing between the paired luminous tracks on one band.**

This ratio is useful visually even if the absolute dimensions remain uncertain.

A rough real-world calibration suggested:

- tube-to-tube center spacing within one band: ~1.6–1.8 ft
- clear spacing from one band pair to the next: ~3 ft

These are very approximate and should not be treated as construction dimensions.

## 15. Photo-derived edge angles

Review of the real white-star photographs suggested the major straight-edge families lie close to:

\[
0^\circ,\ \pm30^\circ,\ \pm60^\circ
\]

The top point appears close to a:

\[
\approx 60^\circ
\]

included apex.

This is much more plausible than an earlier 33° estimate, which should be discarded.

### Current working interpretation

The original full-scale layout may have used construction-friendly 30°/60° geometry.

However, this is still **photo-derived**, not blueprint-confirmed.

---

## 16. Tube placement on each structural band

Close-up photos show the luminous tube runs mounted near the two edges of each metal star member.

Working interpretation for one structural band:

- one tube run near the outer edge
- one tube run near the inner edge
- both follow the same star-shaped structural member
- the two tube runs form a close visible pair

This is the correct physical concept for the reproduction.

Do **not** model the six loops as unrelated independent scaled stars.

Instead:

1. model three structural star bands
2. place two luminous tracks on each band
3. keep the two tracks close together
4. preserve much larger gaps between the three bands

## 17. Good and bad replica references

### Useful secondary reference: illuminated 3D print

A user-provided photo of a 3D-printed illuminated Roanoke Star replica was a surprisingly useful secondary reference.

What it gets right:

- three large nested star groups
- large gaps between structural groups
- white-only presentation
- overall recognizable silhouette

What it gets wrong / oversimplifies:

- decorative segmented light pattern
- likely generic star geometry
- tube placement too stylized
- not suitable as an engineering source

Use it only as a **sanity check for grouping and spacing**, not for exact dimensions.

A downloadable Roanoke Star model was found on MakerWorld.

- exact geometry

## 19. Incorrect approaches that should be avoided

### 19.1 Six evenly spaced nested stars

Rejected.

This destroys the characteristic spacing.

Correct structure:

> close pair / large gap / close pair / large gap / close pair

### 19.2 Regular pentagram geometry

Rejected.

The documented 88.5 × 84.5 ft envelope is incompatible with a regular upright pentagram.

### 19.3 Using RGB photos as the main tube-count reference

Rejected.

RGB configurations can visually hide or merge tracks.

### 19.4 Using image generation for engineering topology

Rejected.

Image generation repeatedly:

- added extra contours
- changed spacing
- altered topology
- distorted the star silhouette

Future engineering work should be numerical / vector / CAD based.

## 20. Recommended reconstruction workflow

### Phase 1 — Outer silhouette

Use the best near-front real photograph and impose:

- vertical symmetry
- overall height = 88.5 ft
- overall width = 84.5 ft

Solve the outer 10-vertex star contour.

### Phase 2 — Structural star frames

Estimate and fit:

- outer frame
- middle frame
- inner frame

Use approximate starting scales:

- 1.00
- 0.74
- 0.43

Then refine against multiple photos.

### Phase 3 — Tube centerlines

For each structural frame:

- derive one outer-edge tube centerline
- derive one inner-edge tube centerline

Preserve:

- small tube-pair spacing
- large inter-frame gaps

### Phase 4 — Enforce symmetry

Mirror measured left-side geometry to the right side rather than trusting photo asymmetry.

### Phase 5 — Compare against multiple reference photos

Overlay the model on:

- white-lit frontal photo
- daylight structure photos
- close-up apex photo
- close-up arm photo
- lower-point photo
- oblique close-ups

Reject any model that only matches one view.

### Phase 6 — CAD / Blender

Once vertex coordinates are stable, generate:

- exact CAD polyline coordinates
- tube path splines
- structural frame profiles
- printable scaled model

## 25. Bottom line

The reproduction should **not** be built as six evenly scaled stars.

It should be built as:

> **three widely separated structural star frames, each carrying two closely paired white illuminated tracks**

with the real outer envelope:

> **~88 ft 6 in high × ~84 ft 6 in wide**

and a vertically elongated, photo-matched silhouette.

The remaining unsolved problem is not the broad topology; that is now fairly clear. The remaining problem is obtaining or reconstructing the **exact 10-vertex geometry and exact internal frame offsets**.

The best path forward is a deterministic photogrammetric / CAD reconstruction from real frontal photographs, followed by archival validation if the original shop or as-built drawings can be located.
